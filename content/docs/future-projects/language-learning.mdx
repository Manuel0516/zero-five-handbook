> **Status: DESIGN PROPOSAL — nothing here is deployed.** This page defines the
> product, architecture, privacy boundary, rollout, and acceptance tests before
> Gamma is changed. The target URL is `https://language.zero-five.space`,
> available only through the Zero Five LAN/WireGuard and AdGuard path, with no
> application login.

## Product vision

Build one personal language-learning workspace for **Swedish** and **Mandarin
Chinese (Simplified characters + Pinyin)**. It should turn real experiences into
structured learning material instead of separating notes, flashcards,
transcripts, pronunciation, and AI chat into unrelated tools.

The core loop is:

```text
speak / record / photograph / write
                │
                ▼
       local extraction pipeline
 transcription · OCR · diarization
                │
                ▼
         AI analysis proposal
 vocabulary · grammar · corrections · cultural knowledge
                │
                ▼
           review and merge
 knowledge tree · notes · examples · pronunciation · practice
                │
                ▼
       level-aware tutor + spaced review
```

The application is not only a chatbot. Its durable product is the learner-owned
knowledge base: every AI suggestion must be traceable to its source and remain
editable, exportable, and useful without the original model.

## Confirmed product decisions

- One owner, no account system and no login screen.
- Reachable only on the home LAN or Zero Five WireGuard through the private
  AdGuard name `language.zero-five.space`; never publish a public DNS record.
- Dark-mode-first, clean monochrome interface using black, white, and gray.
- Appica UI is the preferred React component foundation; use accessible Base UI
  primitives and a small local design-token layer so components can be replaced
  without rewriting product logic.
- Swedish and Mandarin are equal first-class languages. Mandarin content uses
  Simplified Chinese and Pinyin, including tones.
- Conversation language detection is automatic and may identify mixed-language
  passages.
- Transcripts support speaker diarization as Speaker 1, Speaker 2, and so on;
  the owner may rename speakers per conversation.
- Audio is temporary by default. Keep it only when the owner explicitly selects
  **Save audio**. After successful transcription and durable transcript storage,
  unselected source audio is deleted automatically.
- Speech-to-text stays local. Conversation analysis may use a configured cloud
  model. The interface must make the destination and privacy consequence clear
  before sending transcript or image content externally.
- Local Ollama and optional API providers are configurable inside the app.
- Export must support a full portable archive, including only the audio files
  explicitly retained by the owner.
- The first complete release covers all major product areas. Delivery remains
  incremental through end-to-end vertical slices rather than a single untested
  “big bang.”

## Scope of the complete first release

### 1. Home and learning dashboard

- Swedish and Mandarin overview cards: known items, due reviews, recent
  conversations, active topics, and confidence trends.
- Resume actions for an unfinished transcript review, image import, lesson, or
  tutor thread.
- Search/command palette across vocabulary, grammar, notes, transcripts,
  examples, and saved media.
- Clear processing state: queued, transcribing, analyzing, awaiting review,
  completed, or failed.

### 2. Knowledge tree

Each language has a navigable tree with typed, linkable nodes rather than a
folder-only hierarchy:

```text
Language
├── Vocabulary
│   ├── Everyday life
│   ├── Work and study
│   ├── Travel
│   ├── People and relationships
│   └── owner-defined categories
├── Grammar
│   ├── word order / sentence patterns
│   ├── morphology and forms
│   └── particles, tense/aspect, classifiers, etc.
├── Pronunciation
│   ├── Swedish vowels, pitch accent and difficult clusters
│   └── Mandarin initials, finals, tones and tone changes
├── Expressions and pragmatics
├── Culture and context
└── Notes and learning goals
```

A node can have parents and related concepts, examples, tags, source evidence,
mastery state, review history, and attachments. Language-specific fields include:

- **Swedish:** lemma, part of speech, gender/article, plural/definite forms,
  verb forms, pronunciation hint, and examples.
- **Mandarin:** Simplified form, Pinyin with tone marks and tone numbers,
  definition, measure word/classifier, part of speech, character components,
  audio, and examples.

New categories are owner-editable. AI suggestions enter a review inbox before
changing the durable tree; bulk accept is allowed, but silent autonomous edits
are not part of the first release.

### 3. Conversation capture and transcription

The mobile-first recorder is a foreground web/PWA experience:

1. Choose or auto-detect the language context.
2. Optionally select **Save audio** before or after recording.
3. Record with elapsed time, input-level meter, pause/resume, and a persistent
   recording indicator.
4. Upload small recoverable chunks over HTTPS rather than one fragile final
   file.
5. Assemble and normalize locally on Gamma.
6. Transcribe locally with timestamps and language spans.
7. Run speaker diarization and allow speaker renaming.
8. Display an editable transcript before analysis.
9. Analyze for unfamiliar vocabulary, grammar structures, corrections,
   pronunciation targets, useful expressions, and contextual knowledge.
10. Review and merge selected suggestions into the relevant language tree.
11. Delete temporary audio after successful processing unless **Save audio** was
    selected.

The app will not add a consent gate because the owner explicitly declined one.
It must still display an unambiguous recording state and documentation must state
that the owner is responsible for obtaining permission and complying with local
recording law. It must never offer covert or automatic background recording.

A browser recorder is not assumed to survive an iPhone screen lock, OS eviction,
or extended backgrounding. The first release therefore supports foreground
capture and recoverable chunk upload. A native wrapper is a later option only if
real-phone testing proves the web constraint unacceptable.

### 4. Image and handwritten-note ingestion

- Capture from the phone camera or upload JPEG/PNG/HEIC/PDF.
- Keep the original image temporary unless explicitly retained.
- Run local OCR/vision when practical; allow a configured cloud vision model
  only after the UI shows which provider will receive the image.
- Extract candidate words, phrases, translations, grammar notes, and headings.
- Compare candidates with existing knowledge using normalized forms, aliases,
  Chinese script/Pinyin matching, and semantic similarity.
- Present a side-by-side review: original crop, extracted text, duplicates,
  updates, and genuinely new nodes.
- Commit accepted items in one undoable import batch and preserve source
  provenance.

### 5. Level-aware AI tutor

The integrated tutor reads a retrieval-limited view of the selected language's
knowledge graph, recent mistakes, mastery state, and learning goals. It should:

- answer language questions at the owner's current level;
- distinguish “already learned,” “seen but weak,” and “new” concepts;
- generate examples using mostly known material with a controlled amount of new
  vocabulary;
- explain Swedish or Mandarin in English, Swedish, Chinese, or a configurable
  mix;
- cite the internal knowledge nodes and source excerpts used in an answer;
- propose knowledge updates through the same review inbox instead of mutating
  notes directly;
- create drills from real conversations and images without exposing private
  material to a cloud model unless that provider is selected for that task.

### 6. Practice and progress

- Flashcard and cloze review using an FSRS-style scheduler.
- Listening recognition with saved or generated pronunciation audio.
- Dictation, sentence ordering, translation, free response, and conversation
  correction exercises.
- Tone/Pinyin drills for Mandarin and difficult-vowel/pitch-accent notes for
  Swedish.
- Per-item mastery, lapses, confidence, source frequency, and next review.
- Goals and trends without gamified noise or fabricated “fluency scores.”
- A mistake can link back to the transcript, image, lesson, or tutor interaction
  that produced it.

### 7. Settings, models, and portability

- Configure local Ollama endpoint/model roles separately for chat, analysis,
  embeddings, OCR/vision, and optional TTS.
- Configure optional API providers with server-side encrypted secrets; never
  expose keys to the browser or write them into Git, logs, exports, or the
  handbook.
- Per-task routing policy with transcription hard-locked to local execution.
- Model capability test, latency check, and sample preview before saving a
  provider configuration.
- Export a versioned archive containing Markdown, JSON, database metadata,
  attachments, and explicitly saved audio. Include checksums and a documented
  restore test.
- Integrate persistent application data into Delta's existing encrypted backup
  controller after the app is verified.

## UX and visual direction

Use a **Linear-like monochrome workspace**, not a generic colorful education
app:

- near-black page (`#08090a`), dark panels (`#0f1011`/`#191a1b`), near-white
  text, subtle gray borders, and no decorative gradients;
- one restrained neutral/indigo interaction accent only when state cannot be
  communicated clearly in grayscale;
- Inter/Geist-style typography, compact navigation, generous content spacing,
  keyboard command palette, and strong mobile touch targets;
- Appica UI for accessible buttons, dialogs, menus, tabs, forms, drawers,
  tooltips, tables, and progress elements; custom components only for the
  recorder waveform, transcript timeline, knowledge graph, and review diff;
- responsive three-pane desktop workspace (navigation, content, context) that
  collapses to a bottom-navigation and full-screen task flow on phones;
- accessibility baseline: visible focus, keyboard navigation, semantic labels,
  reduced-motion support, sufficient contrast, transcript playback controls,
  and no color-only state.

Primary routes:

```text
/                       dashboard
/learn/[language]       language overview
/knowledge/[language]   tree, graph, table and search views
/capture                 recorder and processing queue
/conversations/[id]     audio/transcript/analysis/review
/import                  image and document ingestion
/practice                due reviews and generated exercises
/tutor                   level-aware agent
/settings/models         local/cloud provider routing
/settings/data           retention, export, restore and deletion
```

## Proposed architecture

```text
iPhone / desktop browser
  │ HTTPS, private DNS, WireGuard/LAN only
  ▼
Delta Traefik + AdGuard
  │ private reverse proxy
  ▼
Gamma: ~/services/language-learning
  ├── web        Next.js + React + TypeScript + Appica UI
  ├── api        FastAPI domain/API service
  ├── worker     Python media, ASR, OCR and AI jobs
  ├── postgres   knowledge, jobs, review history, embeddings
  └── data       temporary media + explicitly retained attachments
       │
       ├── local ASR/diarization/OCR
       ├── Gamma Ollama / existing local model route
       └── optional cloud analysis/vision provider
```

Recommended boundaries:

- **Web:** Next.js App Router, React, TypeScript, Appica UI, Tailwind, Motion only
  where it communicates state, and a PWA manifest/service worker for installable
  mobile access.
- **API:** FastAPI with generated OpenAPI client types. Domain services own
  knowledge mutations, imports, export, and model routing.
- **Database:** PostgreSQL with `pgvector`; an adjacency/closure representation
  supports tree traversal while typed links permit graph relationships. Use
  database migrations from the first commit.
- **Jobs:** a PostgreSQL-backed durable queue (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`) avoids an
  additional Redis service on resource-constrained Gamma. Every job is
  idempotent, retryable, cancellable, and visible in the UI.
- **Media:** host-mounted persistent data below the service directory, with
  separate `tmp`, `retained-audio`, `images`, and `exports` paths. Temporary
  cleanup is both event-driven and periodically reconciled after crashes.
- **AI adapters:** stable interfaces for ASR, diarization, embedding, analysis,
  vision/OCR, chat, and TTS. Store model/provider identity and prompt version
  with every generated artifact.

## Local AI model strategy

`Confucius4-R2T2` is a promising candidate because it is an open-source,
append-only, low-latency streaming ASR model, is optimized for Chinese/English,
and lists Swedish among its additional languages. It is **a candidate, not a
preselected production dependency**: its release is new and Gamma is a small
CPU-only host shared with Minecraft and Ollama.

Before choosing the default ASR engine, benchmark the same owner-recorded Swedish,
Mandarin, English, and mixed-language fixtures against:

1. R2T2 in the smallest practical local runtime/quantization;
2. `faster-whisper` or `whisper.cpp` as the mature fallback;
3. optional post-processing for punctuation and language-span correction.

Measure word/character errors, names, Swedish compounds, Mandarin characters and
Pinyin suitability, mixed-language switching, real-time factor, peak RAM, CPU,
and container cold start. Do not select a model from marketing claims alone.

Speaker labels are a separate concern. Evaluate WhisperX/pyannote-style local
diarization against Gamma's memory and licensing/download requirements. The
architecture must allow transcript and diarization engines to be combined rather
than requiring one model to do both.

Embeddings should use the existing local embedding capability where quality is
sufficient. Analysis/tutoring starts with Gamma's Ollama route but can use an
optional cloud provider selected in app settings. TTS/pronunciation begins with
human-recorded or explicitly generated clips; language-appropriate local voices
must pass a quality test before becoming the default.

## Gamma resource and lifecycle rules

Gamma has about 6.7 GB RAM, 16 GB swap, Minecraft, Open WebUI/Ollama, and other
containers. Therefore:

- deploy the project as its own Compose application under
  `~/services/language-learning` with `restart: unless-stopped` and healthchecks;
- start with Gamma at boot/wake through Docker's existing service lifecycle;
- set hard container memory/CPU limits and one heavy AI job at a time;
- avoid keeping ASR, diarization, and LLM models resident simultaneously;
- add a resource guard that queues heavy work while Minecraft or another model
  makes safe processing impossible, rather than pushing Gamma into swap thrash;
- benchmark with Minecraft both running and stopped, and document whether a
  “heavy processing mode” is required;
- cap temporary upload size and age, monitor disk consumption, and fail closed
  before the filesystem fills;
- make interrupted jobs resumable after Gamma suspends or restarts.

No hardware claim is considered proven until an assembled conversation fixture
records on the phone, survives upload, transcribes, diarizes, analyzes, merges
into knowledge, and produces a practice item on Gamma.

## Network and security boundary

```text
Public internet ──X── language.zero-five.space
LAN/WireGuard → AdGuard private rewrite → Delta Traefik → Gamma service
```

- Add only a private AdGuard rewrite and private Traefik route. Do **not** add a
  public Cloudflare A record.
- TLS is mandatory because mobile microphone/camera APIs require a secure
  context. Follow Delta's existing private-app certificate pattern.
- No login is acceptable only while the network boundary is verified. The API
  must still reject unexpected `Host`/origin values and use same-site CSRF
  protections for mutations.
- Bind backend services to Gamma/Compose networking; do not publish database,
  worker, Ollama, or debugging ports to LAN/WireGuard.
- Validate upload type by content, transcode in a sandboxed worker, reject
  oversized inputs, strip unsafe metadata, and never execute uploaded content.
- Encrypt provider secrets at rest with a host-local key excluded from backups
  unless the recovery procedure explicitly handles it. Redact prompts, content,
  and keys from normal logs.
- Provide complete deletion for conversation media, transcript, derived chunks,
  embeddings, suggestions, and exports. Backups are append-only recovery copies,
  so the UI must explain their separate retention boundary.

## Data model outline

Core entities:

- `languages` and owner preferences;
- `knowledge_nodes`, typed `knowledge_edges`, aliases, examples, attachments,
  source links, proficiency and review state;
- `conversations`, recoverable audio chunks, transcript segments, language spans,
  speakers, and optional retained recording;
- `ingestions` for camera/image/document imports and OCR regions;
- `suggestion_batches` and item-level accept/reject/edit decisions;
- `practice_items`, review logs, schedules, mistakes, and goals;
- `agent_threads`, retrieved node references, model identity, and provider class;
- `jobs`, attempts, progress, errors, cancellation and cleanup state;
- `provider_configs` with encrypted credentials and per-capability routing;
- `audit_events` for knowledge changes, imports, exports, and destructive actions.

Every derived item records its source, model/provider, prompt/pipeline version,
creation time, confidence, and owner review state. Deduplication never destroys
source evidence.

## Delivery plan

### Phase 0 — research fixtures and acceptance baselines

1. Create a private, consented fixture set: short/long Swedish, Mandarin,
   English, mixed-language, two-speaker overlap, background noise, one notebook
   photo, and one printed page.
2. Record expected transcripts and vocabulary/grammar extraction by hand.
3. Benchmark R2T2 and Whisper alternatives locally; test diarization separately.
4. Measure Gamma RAM/CPU/disk with Minecraft and the current AI stack active.
5. Decide the ASR runtime, diarization model, OCR path, file limits, and heavy-job
   concurrency from evidence.
6. Validate Appica UI's required components, license, bundle behavior, dark theme,
   accessibility, and Next.js compatibility before locking the UI layer.

**Gate:** a written benchmark supports the selected local pipeline and shows a
safe Gamma operating envelope.

### Phase 1 — foundation and vertical skeleton

1. Create the repository, Compose topology, healthchecks, migrations, typed API
   contract, test fixtures, and local development setup.
2. Build the monochrome shell, responsive navigation, dashboard, command palette,
   job drawer, error states, and settings structure using Appica UI.
3. Add PostgreSQL, durable jobs, storage abstraction, audit log, retention
   reconciler, and backup/export schema version.
4. Add private-network deployment templates without secrets; perform no public
   DNS work.

**Gate:** the shell survives container restart; a synthetic job resumes; backup
and restore of seed data passes.

### Phase 2 — knowledge, review, and practice core

1. Implement Swedish and Mandarin node schemas, tree/graph/table views, search,
   relations, examples, and provenance.
2. Implement suggestion review, duplicate resolution, bulk accept/reject, undo,
   and audit history.
3. Implement FSRS-style scheduling and the initial exercise types.
4. Add Markdown/JSON export and deterministic import round-trip tests.

**Gate:** a fixture batch imports, deduplicates, becomes reviewable knowledge,
creates due practice, exports, and restores without loss.

### Phase 3 — conversation capture pipeline

1. Build foreground mobile capture, chunk upload, recovery, pause/resume,
   temporary/retained-audio controls, and crash cleanup.
2. Add normalization, selected local ASR adapter, language spans, timestamps,
   diarization, speaker rename, transcript correction, and playback sync.
3. Add local/cloud analysis routing and structured vocabulary/grammar/knowledge
   proposals with source excerpts.
4. Merge accepted suggestions into the knowledge tree and create practice from
   the same conversation.

**Gate:** the full phone-to-practice flow passes on iPhone over LAN and
WireGuard, with temporary audio demonstrably deleted and explicitly retained
audio preserved.

### Phase 4 — image ingestion

1. Add camera/file upload with HEIC/PDF normalization and safe metadata handling.
2. Add local OCR/vision adapter and optional explicit cloud vision route.
3. Implement crop-linked extraction, language normalization, duplicate matching,
   review, undo, and source provenance.
4. Generate practice from accepted handwritten vocabulary.

**Gate:** the notebook-photo fixture adds only approved missing words, preserves
correct Chinese/Pinyin or Swedish forms, and does not duplicate existing nodes.

### Phase 5 — level-aware tutor

1. Implement retrieval over selected knowledge, mastery, recent errors, and
   source material.
2. Add level policy, answer citations, “known/new” labels, context controls, and
   per-thread provider visibility.
3. Add agent-proposed knowledge edits through review; prohibit direct writes.
4. Add conversation-derived lessons and adaptive exercises.

**Gate:** evaluator prompts prove that the tutor uses owned knowledge, cites the
nodes it used, respects provider routing, and cannot silently alter knowledge.

### Phase 6 — production hardening and Gamma deployment

1. Run unit, API contract, migration, job retry, browser, accessibility,
   retention, security, and archive round-trip suites.
2. Deploy under `~/services/language-learning`; verify boot/wake restart,
   healthchecks, resource limits, and interruption recovery.
3. Add the private AdGuard rewrite, Delta Traefik route and certificate only;
   verify the public path cannot reach the service.
4. Enroll persistent paths in Delta's encrypted backups, run a backup, restore to
   an isolated location, and verify checksums plus an application-level restore.
5. Test real iPhone microphone/camera behavior over LAN and WireGuard, including
   screen lock/background limitations, network interruption, Gamma suspend, and
   long recordings.
6. Move verified operational facts into a Gamma runbook page; leave this page as
   the product/design history.

**Gate:** one assembled Swedish and one Mandarin workflow pass end to end after a
Gamma reboot, and a fresh restored instance can open the knowledge tree,
transcripts, review history, practice schedule, and retained audio.

## Test matrix and completion criteria

The platform is not complete until all of these are demonstrated with real
outputs:

- Swedish, Mandarin and mixed-language transcription quality measured against
  the fixture truth; speaker labels editable and stable.
- Temporary audio deletion after success, retained-audio persistence after
  restart/backup/restore, and cleanup after failed/interrupted jobs.
- Vocabulary and grammar suggestions are structured, source-linked, editable,
  deduplicated, and never silently merged.
- Chinese nodes preserve Simplified characters, Pinyin and tones; Swedish nodes
  preserve gender/forms and pronunciation notes.
- Image import identifies missing items without overwriting stronger existing
  knowledge.
- Tutor answers reflect current mastery, cite internal nodes, and visibly state
  whether a local or cloud model handled the request.
- Exercise schedules survive export/import and backup restore.
- Phone UI works with touch and accessibility controls; desktop UI supports
  keyboard navigation.
- Service starts with Gamma, recovers queued work, respects resource limits, and
  does not destabilize Minecraft/current AI services.
- LAN and WireGuard access succeed; a public-origin probe fails before reaching
  the application.
- No API key, transcript, image, audio, or prompt content appears in Git or
  ordinary logs.

## Risks and explicit trade-offs

- **Gamma capacity:** local ASR + diarization + Ollama may exceed practical CPU or
  memory. The benchmark gate may require slower queued processing, stopping
  Minecraft for heavy jobs, a smaller model, or later hardware acceleration.
- **R2T2 maturity:** promising streaming behavior does not prove Swedish or
  mixed-language quality on this hardware. Keep the adapter boundary and mature
  Whisper fallback.
- **Mobile web recording:** iOS may stop microphone work when locked/backgrounded.
  Do not promise all-day passive capture from a PWA; validate foreground capture
  first and consider a native client later.
- **Diarization:** overlap, noise, and similar voices can mislabel speakers.
  Speaker rename and transcript correction are mandatory.
- **Cloud privacy:** analysis can reveal an entire conversation. Cloud execution
  must be explicit, provider-visible, and avoid uploading raw audio because
  transcription is local-only.
- **No login:** security depends completely on the private network and proxy
  boundary. Any future public exposure requires authentication before the route
  changes.
- **Knowledge quality:** automatic ingestion can pollute the learning graph.
  Review-first mutation and provenance cost extra interaction but preserve trust.
- **Recording responsibility:** absence of a consent gate does not remove legal
  or ethical obligations; the owner must obtain any required consent.

## Deferred beyond the first complete release

- covert/background surveillance recording;
- public multi-user accounts, sharing, classrooms, or social features;
- native iOS/Android clients unless PWA recording tests justify them;
- automated voice identification or biometric speaker profiles;
- autonomous knowledge mutation without review;
- cloud speech transcription;
- promises of objective fluency scoring.

## Open decisions for the benchmark/design review

These are deliberately evidence-gated rather than guessed now:

1. R2T2 versus Whisper-family default for Swedish/Mandarin/mixed speech.
2. Diarization runtime and model that fit Gamma without destabilizing services.
3. Local OCR/vision quality for the owner's handwriting and whether cloud vision
   is needed per import.
4. Maximum reliable recording duration and chunk size on the owner's iPhone.
5. Whether local TTS voices are good enough for Swedish and Mandarin
   pronunciation practice.
6. Exact cloud analysis providers and credential recovery policy.
7. Whether “heavy processing mode” stops Minecraft automatically or only asks
   the owner to do so.
